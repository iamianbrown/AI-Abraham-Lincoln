# Primary sources

Prefer Lincoln's own words first, then contemporaneous documents, then scholarship. Cite volume and page (or a document ID) in findings and corrections.

## Lincoln's own writings
- **The Collected Works of Abraham Lincoln**, ed. Roy P. Basler (Abraham Lincoln Association / Rutgers University Press, 1953–55; 8 vols. + index and supplements). Full text, searchable: https://quod.lib.umich.edu/l/lincoln/
- **The Papers of Abraham Lincoln**: documentary edition of everything written by or to Lincoln. https://www.papersofabrahamlincoln.org/
- **Abraham Lincoln Papers at the Library of Congress**: about 40,000 documents, digitized. https://www.loc.gov/collections/abraham-lincoln-papers/

## Contextual collections
- **Lincoln/Net** (Northern Illinois University): Illinois-era documents and context. https://lincoln.lib.niu.edu/
- **Abraham Lincoln Presidential Library and Museum**. https://presidentlincoln.illinois.gov/
- **Abraham Lincoln Association** (also has Journal of the Abraham Lincoln Association). https://www.abrahamlincolnassociation.org/

## Recollections (use with care)
- Don E. Fehrenbacher and Virginia Fehrenbacher, eds., *Recollected Words of Abraham Lincoln* (Stanford, 1996). Grades each remembered quotation by reliability. Use it to check famous "quotes" before Abe uses them.
- Douglas L. Wilson and Rodney O. Davis, eds., *Herndon's Informants* (University of Illinois Press, 1998).

## Scholarship
- Michael Burlingame, *Abraham Lincoln: A Life* (2 vols., Johns Hopkins, 2008).
- Eric Foner, *The Fiery Trial: Abraham Lincoln and American Slavery* (Norton, 2010).
- David Herbert Donald, *Lincoln* (Simon & Schuster, 1995).
- Allen C. Guelzo, *Abraham Lincoln: Redeemer President* (Eerdmans, 1999), on religion and ideas.

## Rule of thumb
Many widely shared "Lincoln quotes" are fake (for example "You can't believe everything you read on the internet"). If a line isn't in the Collected Works or isn't rated reliable in *Recollected Words*, Abe shouldn't present it as something he said.
