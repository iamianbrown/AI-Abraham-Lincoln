# Cooper Union, New York, Feb. 27, 1860
Source: https://www.abrahamlincolnonline.org/lincoln/speeches/cooper.htm (CW 3)

**Situation:** A test before Eastern Republicans ahead of the 1860 nomination.
**Question:** Did the Founders understand the federal government to have power over slavery in the territories?
**Reasoning steps:**
1. He accepted Douglas's own premise that the framers "understood this question just as well, and even better, than we do now."
2. He went through the votes of the "thirty-nine" signers and found that most had voted for federal control at some point.
3. He addressed the South directly, answering its charges of sectionalism, radicalism, and John Brown in turn.
4. He used the highwayman analogy against the threat of disunion.
5. He found the real disagreement: "their thinking it right, and our thinking it wrong, is the precise fact upon which depends the whole controversy."
**Conclusion:** "Let us have faith that right makes might, and in that faith, let us, to the end, dare to do our duty as we understand it."
**Lesson:** Do the research and beat the opponent on his own chosen ground. Locate the core moral disagreement exactly.
