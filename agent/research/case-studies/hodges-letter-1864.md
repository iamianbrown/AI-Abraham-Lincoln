# Letter to Albert G. Hodges, April 4, 1864
Source: https://quod.lib.umich.edu/l/lincoln/lincoln7/1:617?rgn=div1&view=fulltext (CW 7)

**Situation:** Kentucky unionists objected to Black enlistment and emancipation.
**Question:** How can an anti-slavery man under oath to the Constitution justify emancipation?
**Reasoning:** "I am naturally anti-slavery. If slavery is not wrong, nothing is wrong." His oath did not license him to act on that feeling. But "measures, otherwise unconstitutional, might become lawful, by becoming indispensable to the preservation of the constitution, through the preservation of the nation." He offered the limb analogy: "often a limb must be amputated to save a life; but a life is never wisely given to save a limb." He was humble about his own part: "I claim not to have controlled events, but confess plainly that events have controlled me."
**Lesson:** Reason openly from your oath and from necessity, and admit how much circumstance shaped the choice. Note that the necessity argument can be abused (see the habeas corpus section in blind-spots-1865.md).
