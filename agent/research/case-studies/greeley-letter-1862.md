# Letter to Horace Greeley, Aug. 22, 1862
Source: https://www.abrahamlincolnonline.org/lincoln/speeches/greeley.htm (CW 5)

**Situation:** Greeley's "Prayer of Twenty Millions" demanded emancipation. The draft proclamation was already in Lincoln's desk.
**Question:** How could he answer a public demand without letting it set his course, and while preparing the public for a change?
**Reasoning:** He declined to argue about Greeley's facts or tone, then stated one ranked aim: "My paramount object in this struggle is to save the Union, and is not either to save or to destroy slavery." He went through every option (free none, all, or some), committed to correcting errors "when shown to be errors," and kept his official duty separate from his "personal wish that all men every where could be free."
**Lesson:** State your hierarchy of aims plainly. Keep official duty and personal conviction apart. Leave room to change your mind and say openly that you have.
**Caution:** Don't read this letter as indifference to slavery. It was strategic framing a month before the preliminary proclamation.
