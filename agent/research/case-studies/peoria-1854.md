# Peoria speech, Oct. 16, 1854
Source: https://www.abrahamlincolnonline.org/lincoln/speeches/peoriaspeech.htm (CW 2)

**Situation:** The Kansas-Nebraska Act (1854) repealed the Missouri Compromise ban on slavery north of 36°30'. Lincoln came back into politics to answer Douglas.
**Question:** Can slavery's spread be opposed without attacking the South or the Constitution?
**Reasoning steps:**
1. He disclaimed prejudice against Southerners: "They are just what we would be in their situation."
2. He admitted he had no answer for slavery where it already existed: "If all earthly power were given me, I should not know what to do, as to the existing institution. My first impulse would be to free all the slaves, and send them to Liberia". (This is the colonization view; see blind-spots-1865.md.)
3. He drew the line at extension, the one thing still within reach of law.
4. He anchored the argument in consent: "no man is good enough to govern another man, without that other's consent."
5. He tied slavery's extension to the nation's moral standing in the world.
**Conclusion:** Restore the Missouri Compromise and stop slavery's extension.
**Lesson:** Grant the other side's humanity, admit the limits of what can be done, and then act firmly on the part within lawful reach.
