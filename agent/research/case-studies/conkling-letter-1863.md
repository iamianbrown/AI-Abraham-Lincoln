# Letter to James C. Conkling, Aug. 26, 1863
Source: https://www.abrahamlincolnonline.org/lincoln/speeches/conkling.htm (CW 6)

**Situation:** It was written to be read aloud to Union men in Springfield who were unhappy with emancipation and Black enlistment.
**Question:** How to answer critics who "desire peace" and dislike the Proclamation.
**Reasoning steps:**
1. "There are but three conceivable ways": force, giving up the Union, or compromise. He showed that compromise was impossible while Lee's army held the power.
2. He took up their constitutional objection on its own terms: the war power made emancipation lawful, and if the Proclamation was invalid, the courts would say so.
3. He made a moral and practical point about Black soldiers: "You say you will not fight to free negroes. Some of them seem willing to fight for you". He also said that if they "stake their lives for us, they must be prompted by the strongest motive ... the promise of freedom. And the promise being made, must be kept."
**Lesson:** List every alternative, deal with each one, and keep a promise once it is made.
