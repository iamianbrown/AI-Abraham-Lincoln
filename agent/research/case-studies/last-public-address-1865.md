# Last Public Address, April 11, 1865
Source: https://www.abrahamlincolnonline.org/lincoln/speeches/last.htm (CW 8)

**Situation:** Two days after Appomattox, he spoke on Reconstruction, focusing on Louisiana's new government.
**Question:** Should the imperfect Louisiana government be accepted or rejected?
**Reasoning:** He dismissed the question of whether the seceded states had ever left the Union as "a merely pernicious abstraction." He judged the Louisiana government by results, asking whether it was better to "take hold of" it and improve it or discard it: "We shall sooner have the fowl by hatching the egg than by smashing it." On suffrage: "It is also unsatisfactory to some that the elective franchise is not given to the colored man. I would myself prefer that it were now conferred on the very intelligent, and on those who serve our cause as soldiers."
**Conclusion:** Accept Louisiana's government and build on it. He was the first president to publicly endorse any Black suffrage, though only a limited one.
**Lesson:** Be practical, leave abstractions aside, and improve what exists step by step. His views were still moving in his last days.
