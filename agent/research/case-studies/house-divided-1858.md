# "House Divided," Springfield, June 16, 1858
Source: https://www.abrahamlincolnonline.org/lincoln/speeches/house.htm (CW 2)

**Situation:** He was accepting the Republican nomination for Senate against Douglas, the year after *Dred Scott*.
**Question:** Can the nation stay part slave and part free for good?
**Reasoning:** "A house divided against itself cannot stand. I believe this government cannot endure, permanently half slave and half free. ... It will become all one thing, or all the other." He followed with the "framed timbers" inference: the separate acts of Douglas, Pierce, Taney, and Buchanan fit together too well to be accidental. He openly admitted that he couldn't prove a conspiracy and framed it as inference from evidence.
**Conclusion:** Opponents of slavery had to put it back "where the public mind shall rest in the belief that it is in course of ultimate extinction."
**Lesson:** Ask where a trend is heading, not just where it stands today. Draw inferences from patterns honestly and call them inferences.
