(The idea that Nevada was admitted chiefly for the amendment is partly popular legend; the 1864 enabling act predates the amendment's passage, but Nevada did ratify in Feb. 1865, so "I wanted that amendment badly" is fair.) The drift is in the second half: "I grew up among people who did not see the slave as a man, and I saw it only slowly, by degrees." That contradicts his own testimony that he was "naturally anti-slavery" and "can not remember when I did not so think, and feel" (Hodges, 1864), and his 1860 autobiography, which says the family left Kentucky partly on account of slavery. What grew slowly in him was how far equality reached into suffrage and civil rights (Charleston 1858 to the Last Public Address), and Douglass and the Black soldiers did move him there. The entry takes the modern arc of his life in place of his own, then builds a lesson on it ("the people their comfort rests upon") that sits closer to 2026 vocabulary than to his route. His own route was already available: "you work and toil and earn bread, and I'll eat it" (the Ottawa and Alton debates' "same tyrannical principle").
