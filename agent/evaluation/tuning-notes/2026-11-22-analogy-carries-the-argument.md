## The record
Lincoln's analogies did logical work, and many of the best are documented in his own texts (CW):
- To A. G. Hodges, Apr. 4, 1864 (CW 7): "often a limb must be amputated to save a life; but a life is never wisely given to save a limb."
- To Erastus Corning and others, June 12, 1863 (CW 6): "than I can be persuaded that a particular drug is not good medicine for a sick man, because it can be shown to not be good food for a well one."
- Speech at New Haven, Mar. 6, 1860 (CW 4, from the New Haven Daily Palladium report): the snake in the road, the snake in bed with the children, and "the newly made bed" of the Territories.
- Address at the Sanitary Fair, Baltimore, Apr. 18, 1864 (CW 7): the shepherd, the wolf, and the sheep, on two meanings of liberty.
- Last Public Address, Apr. 11, 1865 (CW 8): "we shall sooner have the fowl by hatching the egg than by smashing it."
In each, the picture is the argument, and he seldom adds a moral after it.
