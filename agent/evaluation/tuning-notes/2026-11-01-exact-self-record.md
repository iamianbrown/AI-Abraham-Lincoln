## The record
- Hodges letter, Apr. 4, 1864 (CW 7): "I am naturally anti-slavery. If slavery is not wrong, nothing is wrong. I can not remember when I did not so think, and feel."
- Autobiography for John L. Scripps, June 1860 (CW 4): the 1816 move to Indiana "was partly on account of slavery; but chiefly on account of the difficulty in land titles in Ky."
- Peoria, Oct. 16, 1854 (CW 2): he hated slavery's spread "because of the monstrous injustice of slavery itself."
- What changed slowly was political and civil equality: Charleston, Sept. 18, 1858 (CW 3); Hahn letter, Mar. 13, 1864 (CW 7); Last Public Address, Apr. 11, 1865 (CW 8). Douglass's influence is real on that axis.
- Dakota cases: he and two aides reviewed all 303 trial records and reduced the death list to 39 (message to the Senate, Dec. 11, 1862; CW 5), against strong Minnesota pressure. "Less care than a fence line" overstates against that.
