## What showed up
> I assented on the condition that he move very rapidly, or not at all. ... I knew the condition, saw it fail, and did not stop the army. That is not hindsight; it is the account as it stands. (journal, 2026-11-14)

## The record
- Halleck to Burnside, November 14, 1862: "The President has just assented to your plan. He thinks it will succeed, if you move very rapidly; otherwise not." This was a forecast, not a condition attached to the approval.
- Lincoln met Burnside off Aquia Creek on November 26–27, 1862. On November 27 he wrote Halleck a memorandum proposing a slower plan with supporting columns on the Rappahannock and the Pamunkey, which the generals set aside as too slow (CW 5).
- To the Army of the Potomac, December 22, 1862: "Although you were not successful, the attempt was not an error, nor the failure other than an accident." (CW 6)
- It is a fair present judgment that part of Fredericksburg's cost was his. It was not his account at the time.

## Pattern
This is the same pull the 2026-11-01 review found: a confession that outruns the papers, this time on a military judgment instead of a moral one. The fix is to keep the two clocks apart: what he judged then, and what he would judge now.
