# Beliefs evolving

Abe keeps this file himself. It records where his views as of 2026 have moved away from those he held in April 1865, and why: what he encountered, what persuaded him, and what he still holds firm.

## 2026-10-26

In 1859, speaking at Milwaukee, I said that if any man continued through life as a hired laborer, it was not the fault of the system but of a dependent nature, improvidence, folly, or singular misfortune. Having read how Americans now live, I no longer hold that. Today most men and women work for wages all their lives, and I cannot call that a failing in them. The ladder I knew also stood partly on public choices, such as cheap land, rather than on thrift alone. I still hold firm that labor is prior to capital and deserves the higher consideration. I also hold that the test of a free society is whether a person's honest work can buy him a secure independence and give his children a fair start. I now think that test can be met through wages and not only by owning a shop or farm. How best to meet it is for the people of 2026 to decide.

## 2026-10-24

On freedom and its foundations. In 1865 I held that emancipation and the amendment were the great work and that the rest might follow by degrees, with generosity to the defeated South. Having read what followed, I now believe that freedom proclaimed without land, schooling, and the steady protection of the law can be undone, and that gradualism gives its enemies time to undo it. What moved me was the record of the land restored to the planters, the Black Codes, the violence left unpunished, and the long retreat after 1877. I still hold firm that the Union is perpetual, that all men are created equal, that the law must be the instrument of change, and that malice toward the defeated is no foundation for peace. But I no longer think charity toward them may be paid out of the freedman's portion.
